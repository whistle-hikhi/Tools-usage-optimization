# Agent Orchestration/Reasoning Frameworks and Tool-Use Efficiency

## How do ReAct, Toolformer, Reflexion, LATS, Plan-and-Solve, AutoGPT-style loops, ReWOO, and LLMCompiler work, and what are their efficiency properties vs. accuracy on standard benchmarks?

### Takeaway
These frameworks span a spectrum from single-pass/decoupled tool use (Toolformer, ReWOO, LLMCompiler) to interleaved reasoning-acting (ReAct) to deliberative, multi-trial or tree-search methods (Reflexion, LATS); accuracy generally rises with more LLM calls/tokens spent per task, but the relationship is not linear or guaranteed — some "lean" methods match or beat "heavy" ones on specific benchmarks at a fraction of the cost.

### Cited Findings
- **ReAct** (Yao et al., Oct 2022, arXiv:2210.03629) interleaves verbal reasoning traces with actions against external tools/environments in a single loop; evaluated on HotpotQA, Fever, ALFWorld, WebShop. On ALFWorld and WebShop, 1-2 shot ReAct prompting outperformed imitation/RL baselines trained on 10³–10⁵ task instances, with absolute success-rate improvements of 34% and 10% respectively. — [arXiv:2210.03629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)
- **Toolformer** (Schick et al., Meta AI, Feb 2023, arXiv:2302.04761) self-supervises a model to decide which APIs to call, when, with what arguments, and how to fold results into prediction, using only a handful of demonstrations per tool (calculator, Q&A, search, translation, calendar). It achieves "substantially improved zero-shot performance across downstream tasks, often competitive with much larger models," without degrading core language modeling. — [arXiv:2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)
- **Reflexion** (Shinn et al., Mar 2023, last revised Oct 2023, arXiv:2303.11366) adds verbal self-reflection stored in an episodic memory buffer (no weight updates) across sequential decision-making, coding, and reasoning tasks. Reflexion reports 91% pass@1 on HumanEval, versus GPT-4's 80% baseline at the time. — [arXiv:2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366)
- **LATS** (Zhou et al., Oct 2023, arXiv:2310.04406) unifies reasoning, acting, and planning via MCTS-style tree search over an external environment. Reported results: HumanEval pass@1 92.7% with GPT-4 (83.8% with GPT-3.5); HotPotQA exact match 0.63 for LATS vs. 0.32 for ReAct (and 0.71 for combined CoT+ReAct in some reported comparisons); WebShop score 75.9 (38.0% success), comparable to gradient-based fine-tuning results on WebShop with GPT-3.5. — [arXiv:2310.04406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04406)
- **Plan-and-Solve prompting** (Wang, Xu, Lan, Hu, Lan, Lee, Lim; ACL 2023, arXiv:2305.04091) separates "devise a plan" (decompose into subtasks) from "carry out the plan" (solve each subtask) in a zero-shot CoT setting, targeting calculation, missing-step, and semantic-understanding errors. It "consistently outperforms Zero-shot-CoT across all datasets by a large margin," is comparable to/exceeds Zero-shot-PoT, and approaches 8-shot CoT on math reasoning with GPT-3. — [arXiv:2305.04091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04091)
- **ReWOO** (Xu, Peng et al., May 2023, arXiv:2305.18323) decouples a Planner (produces a full blueprint of interlinked plans before any tool call), a Worker (executes tool calls to gather evidence), and a Solver (pairs plans+evidence to produce the final answer) — reasoning is never interleaved with tool-call waiting. This shifts token cost from quadratic to linear in the number of reasoning steps, yielding 5× token efficiency and a 4% accuracy improvement on HotpotQA versus interleaved (ReAct-style) baselines. ReWOO also enables distilling reasoning from a 175B GPT-3.5 into a fine-tuned 7B LLaMA. — [arXiv:2305.18323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18323) / [GitHub](https://github.com/billxbf/ReWOO)
- **LLMCompiler** (Kim et al., Dec 2023, arXiv:2312.04511, ICML 2024) uses a Function Calling Planner (builds a DAG of tasks), a Task Fetching Unit (dispatches ready tasks), and an Executor (runs tool calls in parallel wherever the DAG allows). Reported gains vs. ReAct: up to 3.7× latency speedup, up to 6.7× cost reduction, and up to ~9% accuracy improvement; it is also up to 1.35× faster than OpenAI's native parallel function calling at similar accuracy. — [arXiv:2312.04511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04511)
- **AutoGPT-style autonomous loops** (open-ended, no single paper) are repeatedly documented as prone to single-tool loops (same tool/near-identical args repeated) and multi-tool cyclic loops (circular re-visiting of prior states/queries) that degrade reasoning efficiency and flood context with redundant information; a single unattended run can "rack up hundreds of dollars in token usage" if it doesn't self-terminate. Teams moving such loops to production report "lack of control... leading to unreliable autonomy, high costs, and hallucinated outputs." — [vectara/awesome-agent-failures](https://github.com/vectara/awesome-agent-failures/blob/main/docs/case-studies/autogpt-planning-failures.md); general critique summarized via web search (no single peer-reviewed source identified for AutoGPT specifically — treat as practitioner/secondary-source consensus, not a controlled benchmark result).

### Inferences
- The "decouple planning from tool-waiting" idea (ReWOO's Planner/Worker/Solver; LLMCompiler's Planner/Fetcher/Executor) is the common mechanism behind the biggest efficiency wins (5× tokens, 3.7–6.7× latency/cost) — both avoid the sequential think→call→wait→think loop that ReAct and AutoGPT-style agents use natively.
- Accuracy gains from deliberation (Reflexion's self-reflection, LATS's tree search) appear concentrated on tasks with a clear, cheap verifier/reward signal (e.g., unit tests in HumanEval, exact-match QA) — where the extra compute buys information; on tasks without that structure, the deliberation overhead may not pay for itself (see cost-controlled HumanEval results in the next section).
- AutoGPT-style fully autonomous loops appear to be the efficiency "worst case" among these patterns precisely because they lack ReWOO/LLMCompiler's upfront planning structure and Reflexion/LATS's bounded trial budget — nothing caps the number of redundant steps.

### Gaps
- No controlled, apples-to-apples benchmark comparing ReAct, Toolformer, Reflexion, LATS, ReWOO, and LLMCompiler all on one shared task suite with matched models was found; each framework's reported numbers come from its own paper's chosen benchmark subset, limiting direct cross-framework comparability (partially addressed in the next section via the Princeton "AI Agents That Matter" cost-controlled study, but that only covers a subset: LATS, Reflexion, LDB, and a "warming" baseline on HumanEval).
- Could not find a primary peer-reviewed paper quantifying AutoGPT's tool-call counts or token overhead on a standard benchmark (e.g., AgentBench, GAIA) — evidence is secondary/practitioner commentary rather than controlled measurement.
- Toolformer's paper-reported efficiency metrics (tokens/task, latency) were not surfaced in search results; only qualitative "substantially improved zero-shot performance" language was found — exact accuracy numbers and tool-call counts are a gap.

## What is the tradeoff between reasoning-heavy approaches (LATS, Reflexion, multi-step deliberation) and lean approaches (ReWOO, planner-executor, single-pass tool calls)?

### Takeaway
Reasoning-heavy methods buy accuracy with large, often superlinear increases in LLM calls/cost (LATS: 5–20× more LLM calls than ReAct; Reflexion-style loops can reach up to ~2,000 API calls per task in some deployments), while lean/decoupled methods (ReWOO, LLMCompiler, simple retry "warming" baselines) can match or exceed heavy methods' accuracy at a small fraction of the cost on several benchmarks — meaning the heavy approaches are frequently not on the Pareto frontier once cost is controlled for.

### Cited Findings
- LATS requires "5-20x more LLM calls than simpler approaches like ReAct," reflecting its tree-rollout/MCTS structure; it has "the same sample complexity as other tree-based search methods" and can expand fewer nodes on success (i.e., it is not maximally wasteful relative to other tree-search methods), but it still carries "higher computational cost compared to simpler prompting methods like ReAct or Reflexion, which may limit its practicality." — [Agentic Patterns: LATS](https://www.agentic-patterns.com/patterns/language-agent-tree-search-lats/); [arXiv:2310.04406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.04406)
- On a cost-controlled HumanEval comparison (Kapoor, Stroebl, Siegel, Nadgir, Narayanan — Princeton, "AI Agents That Matter," July 2024, arXiv:2407.01502): LATS reached 88% accuracy at **$134.50** total cost; Reflexion reached 87.8% accuracy at **$3.90**; LDB reached 91% at **$2.19**; a trivial "warming" baseline (just gradually raising sampling temperature and retrying) reached **93.2%** accuracy at **$2.45**. The paper's conclusion: "state-of-the-art agent architectures for HumanEval do not outperform simple baselines" once cost is controlled. — [arXiv:2407.01502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)
- ReWOO's decoupled design moves token cost scaling from quadratic to linear in the number of reasoning steps, delivering 5× token efficiency and +4% accuracy on HotpotQA vs. interleaved baselines — demonstrating a lean method beating accuracy, not just cost, on at least one benchmark. — [arXiv:2305.18323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18323)
- LLMCompiler (parallel planner-executor) achieves up to 3.7× latency reduction and 6.7× cost reduction vs. ReAct while also improving accuracy by up to ~9% — another case where "lean" (here, parallelized rather than purely sequential) beats "heavy" (ReAct's sequential interleaving) on both axes simultaneously. — [arXiv:2312.04511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04511)
- In a financial-document-extraction benchmarking study (Kulkarni & Kulkarni, NYU, Mar 2026, arXiv:2603.22651 — note: not a widely-cited/established venue, treat as a single preliminary study) comparing four custom orchestration patterns across GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3 70B, and Mixtral 8x22B on 10,000 SEC filings: a "Reflexive Self-Correcting Loop" architecture achieved the highest field-level F1 (0.936–0.943) but at the highest cost ($0.072–$0.430/doc, 2.30× the sequential baseline) and highest latency (p50 36.4–74.1s, with a p99/p50 ratio of 3.34×, and consumed 2.2× more input tokens than sequential). A "Hierarchical Supervisor-Worker" architecture reached 98.5% of the reflexive architecture's F1 (0.929 vs. 0.943) at only 60.7% of its cost. A hybrid "Hierarchical-Optimized" configuration (semantic caching + model routing + adaptive retry) recovered 89% of the sequential-to-reflexive accuracy gap at only 1.15× the sequential baseline cost. The reflexive architecture was also reported to degrade fastest at scale (beyond ~25K docs/day) as F1 fell from 0.943 at 1K docs/day to 0.871 at 100K docs/day, worse than sequential's degradation (0.903→0.886). — [arXiv:2603.22651](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22651)
- A related aggregate claim (same search thread, source not fully verified beyond the above paper and secondary blog coverage) states that comparing ReAct-GPT4, ReAct-GPT-o3, Reflexion, Plan-Execute, Toolformer, and a domain-tuned agent, "accuracy-optimal configurations cost 4.4–10.8× more than Pareto-efficient alternatives," and that Reflexion's 74.1% efficacy cost 5.12× more than ReAct-GPT-o3's 68.7% efficacy — a 5.4 percentage-point gain for a 1,551% cost increase — while a Plan-Execute architecture reached 71.9% efficacy (close to Reflexion's 74.1%) at 4.1× lower cost with better reliability (64.5% vs. 61.2% pass@8). This appears to be drawn from the same or a closely related financial-benchmarking line of work; exact primary source/benchmark identity could not be independently confirmed in the time available. — reported via web search synthesis, traced toward [arXiv:2603.22651](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22651) and related sources.
- On GAIA, token/cost usage varies hugely by model even holding the agent loop roughly constant: GPT-4o used ~1,380 tokens/task (27,611 total across tasks) and $0.079 total, while Gemini 2.5 Flash used ~5,300 tokens/task (106,019 total) and $0.19 total — illustrating that "a high GAIA score achieved with an enormous number of tool calls is a different product from the same score achieved cheaply." — [Hugging Face GAIA lessons-learned blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/hetline/lessons-learned-on-gaia-agents)

### Inferences
- The accuracy-per-dollar curve for deliberation-heavy methods (LATS, Reflexion) appears steeply diminishing-return once a cheap self-consistency/retry baseline is available — on HumanEval specifically, naive retry-with-temperature-warming dominates both LATS and Reflexion on the Pareto frontier.
- Lean/decoupled architectures are not strictly "cheap but less accurate" — ReWOO and LLMCompiler both report simultaneous accuracy AND efficiency wins over interleaved ReAct-style baselines, suggesting that at least part of ReAct's accuracy cost comes from redundant context/repeated prompting rather than from genuinely needed deliberation.
- Reasoning-heavy architectures seem particularly vulnerable to cost/latency blow-up at scale (the financial-extraction study's reflexive loop degrading fastest under high throughput), which matters for workshop-paper framing around production deployment, not just single-task benchmark accuracy.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the "4.4–10.8× cost for accuracy-optimal configs" and "Reflexion 74.1% vs ReAct-GPT-o3 68.7%" statistics against a clearly identified, citable primary paper distinct from arXiv:2603.22651; this is flagged as a lower-confidence secondary finding and should be re-verified against the primary source before being used as a headline statistic in the final report.
- The NYU financial-document paper (arXiv:2603.22651) uses custom architectures "similar to" named paradigms rather than literal ReAct/Reflexion/LATS implementations — so its numbers are suggestive analogues, not direct measurements of the canonical frameworks.
- No benchmark was found that places ReWOO, LLMCompiler, LATS, and Reflexion all on one Pareto chart with a shared task set and shared base model — the "lean vs. heavy" comparisons above are reconstructed across separate benchmarks (HotpotQA, HumanEval, GAIA, SEC filings) with different base models, so efficiency ratios are not perfectly comparable to one another.

## Are there direct empirical comparisons (in papers) of these frameworks on the same benchmark reporting both accuracy AND efficiency metrics?

### Takeaway
Yes, but direct same-benchmark, multi-framework, cost-and-accuracy comparisons are rare and concentrated in a few sources: Kapoor et al.'s "AI Agents That Matter" (LATS vs. Reflexion vs. LDB vs. a simple retry baseline on HumanEval, plus DSPy cost/accuracy joint optimization on HotPotQA), the LLMCompiler paper (vs. ReAct and OpenAI parallel function calling), the ReWOO paper (vs. interleaved baselines on HotpotQA), and τ-bench / GAIA / Holistic Agent Leaderboard (HAL), which are explicitly designed to report cost alongside accuracy across many agents/models rather than comparing named reasoning frameworks per se.

### Cited Findings
- **"AI Agents That Matter"** (Kapoor et al., Princeton, Jul 2024, arXiv:2407.01502) is the clearest primary source directly comparing named frameworks on one benchmark with both axes reported: on HumanEval, LATS (88% acc., $134.50), Reflexion (87.8% acc., $3.90), LDB (91% acc., $2.19), and a "warming" retry baseline (93.2% acc., $2.45). The paper explicitly argues that "agent evaluations must be cost-controlled; otherwise it will encourage researchers to develop extremely costly agents," and separately shows that jointly optimizing cost and accuracy via a modified DSPy pipeline cut variable cost 53% (GPT-3.5) and 41% (Llama-3-70B) on HotPotQA while preserving accuracy. It also reports finding implementation bugs in both LATS's and STeP's benchmark harnesses that marked incorrect solutions as correct, which is a methodological caution for anyone citing raw framework-vs-framework numbers from other papers. — [arXiv:2407.01502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)
- **LLMCompiler** reports a direct, same-task comparison against ReAct and against OpenAI's native parallel function calling, with all three metrics (latency, cost, accuracy) on the same axis: up to 3.7× latency speedup and 6.7× cost reduction vs. ReAct, ~9% accuracy improvement vs. ReAct, and 1.35× latency improvement over OpenAI parallel function calling at similar accuracy. — [arXiv:2312.04511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04511)
- **ReWOO** reports a direct comparison against interleaved (ReAct-style) reasoning on HotpotQA: 5× token efficiency, +4% accuracy. — [arXiv:2305.18323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18323)
- **τ-bench (tau-bench)** is explicitly built to report both: on the Airline benchmark (50 tasks), evaluation cost ranged $22–$180 across agents (avg. $0.44/task), and agents are explicitly ranked by whether they sit on the accuracy-cost Pareto frontier rather than by accuracy alone; the benchmark's primary metric is pass@1 end-to-end task completion (harder than individual function-call-accuracy benchmarks like BFCL v4 because it requires state tracking across turns and correct task-completion detection). — [HAL: τ-bench tool calling](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/agent/TAU-bench%20Tool%20Calling); [Holistic Agent Leaderboard, arXiv:2510.11977](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11977)
- **GAIA** leaderboard data (as of Aug 2026) shows top model GPT-5 Mini at 44.8% accuracy, Gemini 2.5 Pro at 33.3%, R1 0528 at 27.9%, against a human baseline of ~92%; separately, token/cost accounting (GPT-4o: ~1,380 tokens/task, $0.079 total vs. Gemini 2.5 Flash: ~5,300 tokens/task, $0.19 total) is reported for the same benchmark, allowing accuracy-vs-cost comparison across models/agents on identical tasks, though these are model comparisons more than named-framework comparisons. — [GAIA leaderboard](https://pricepertoken.com/leaderboards/benchmark/gaia); [GAIA paper, arXiv:2311.12983](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.12983); [HF GAIA lessons-learned blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/hetline/lessons-learned-on-gaia-agents)
- **AgentBench** (Liu et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv:2308.03688) evaluates 29 API-based and open-source LLMs as agents across 8 environments, finding "poor long-term reasoning, decision-making, and instruction-following abilities" as the main obstacles, and a significant gap between top commercial LLMs and most open-source models ≤70B — but the search results surfaced did not show AgentBench reporting token/cost metrics alongside accuracy as a primary axis (accuracy/success-rate-centric rather than cost-centric). — [arXiv:2308.03688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03688)
- The **Holistic Agent Leaderboard (HAL)** (arXiv:2510.11977) is framed explicitly as "the missing infrastructure for AI agent evaluation," suggesting that standardized joint cost/accuracy reporting across agent frameworks is still an open infrastructure problem as of its publication — corroborating the gap "AI Agents That Matter" identified in 2024. — [arXiv:2510.11977](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11977)

### Inferences
- The strongest, most citable "same benchmark, multiple named frameworks, both axes reported" evidence for a workshop paper is the Kapoor et al. HumanEval table (LATS/Reflexion/LDB/warming-baseline) — this is likely the single most load-bearing comparison for an accuracy-vs-efficiency argument in tool-use/agent papers.
- The field is moving toward benchmark infrastructure (τ-bench, HAL) that bakes in cost reporting by design, rather than relying on individual framework papers to self-report cost — this is itself a citable trend for a workshop paper's "state of evaluation practice" section.
- Framework-original papers (ReWOO, LLMCompiler) tend to report favorable comparisons against ReAct specifically (the most common "incumbent" baseline), rather than against each other or against Reflexion/LATS — meaning a fully symmetric N-way comparison matrix across all 8 frameworks in the research objective does not exist in the literature as a single source; it must be synthesized (as attempted here) from several papers with different benchmarks/models.

### Gaps
- No single paper was found that runs ReAct, Toolformer, Reflexion, LATS, Plan-and-Solve, ReWOO, and LLMCompiler all on one shared benchmark with one shared base model reporting accuracy + tokens + latency + $ cost for all seven — this remains a genuine gap in the literature (and could be framed as a contribution opportunity for the workshop paper itself).
- Toolformer was not found in any direct cost/accuracy comparison against the other seven frameworks in the sources gathered; its evaluation methodology (zero-shot downstream task performance) is not framed in the same terms (tool-call counts, $ cost) as the agent-benchmark literature (GAIA, τ-bench, AgentBench), likely because Toolformer predates the "agent" framing and is evaluated more like a model capability than an orchestration pattern.
- AgentBench's original paper does not appear (per available search results) to report cost/latency in its headline results, limiting its use for the efficiency side of this report; would need to check the AgentBench GitHub repo or follow-on papers for cost breakdowns if needed.

## What architectural separations have been proposed to cut cost while preserving accuracy (e.g., separating planning from execution, cheap model for tool selection + expensive model for synthesis)?

### Takeaway
The dominant proposed pattern across both academic papers and practitioner sources is a two (or more)-tier model/role separation — decoupling a planning/tool-selection stage (handled by a cheap/fast model or lightweight decision model) from an execution and/or final-synthesis stage (reserved for an expensive frontier model) — layered with techniques like semantic caching, parallel execution, and adaptive retry; reported savings range from roughly 40–90% cost reduction with accuracy preserved or only modestly reduced, though rigorous, peer-reviewed quantification specific to the "cheap tool-selector + expensive synthesizer" split is thinner than the qualitative consensus suggests.

### Cited Findings
- **ReWOO's three-module separation** (Planner / Worker / Solver) is itself an architectural-separation strategy: the Planner produces an interlinked blueprint before any tool call; the Worker executes tools; the Solver synthesizes evidence into the final answer — and crucially, this separation is what enables ReWOO's authors to distill the reasoning capability out of a 175B GPT-3.5 into a fine-tuned 7B LLaMA, i.e., swap in a much smaller model for part of the pipeline. — [arXiv:2305.18323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18323)
- **LLMCompiler's Planner / Task Fetching Unit / Executor separation** achieves parallel execution specifically because planning (building the task DAG) is decoupled from execution (running tool calls); this decoupling is presented as the direct cause of the 3.7× latency and 6.7× cost reductions over ReAct. — [arXiv:2312.04511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04511)
- **"Efficient Agents: Building Effective Agents While Reducing Cost"** (Wang, Hu, Liu, Zhu et al., OPPO, Aug 2025, arXiv:2508.02694) explicitly proposes (1) dynamic model routing where cheaper models handle initial processing/routing while expensive frontier models (GPT-4, Claude) are reserved for complex reasoning/synthesis; (2) a two-stage Planner-Executor architecture where a cheaper model generates task decompositions/tool sequences and an efficient model executes steps, escalating to an expensive model only for complex reasoning; (3) lightweight models performing tool-selection filtering before expensive reasoning is invoked to cut token consumption on irrelevant context. It evaluates on GAIA, BrowseComp, and custom multi-agent benchmarks, reporting (qualitatively, per available extraction) "significant savings through selective expensive model deployment" while "maintaining performance comparable to all-expensive-model baselines" — exact percentage figures could not be extracted from the fetched content (PDF rendering issue). — [arXiv:2508.02694](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02694)
- Practitioner/secondary sources converge on similar numbers: one source claims "most AI agents spend 75 percent of their LLM calls on tiny tool-pick decisions that do not need a premium model," and that routing those to a cheap model (e.g., Haiku-class, DeepSeek V3, open 120B models) while reserving premium models for synthesis yields "70 to 80 percent cost reductions without a measurable quality drop." Another cites an OpenAI-reported case of a 54% cost cut from classifying/routing jobs before model selection. A third estimates "50–90% savings over single-model approaches... realistic for workflows with high volume and a mix of simple and complex tasks." These are blog/vendor-content sources, not peer-reviewed, and should be treated as directional/practitioner consensus rather than rigorously measured results. — [DEV Community: decision models for tool selection](https://dev.to/aws/cut-ai-agent-token-costs-with-decision-models-for-tool-selection-2320); [Roborhythms: cheap router + premium synthesis](https://www.roborhythms.com/cheap-router-premium-synthesis-ai-agent/); [Mindstudio: smart model directs cheaper sub-agents](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/ai-model-orchestration-smart-model-cheaper-sub-agents)
- The "AI Agents That Matter" paper's jointly-optimized-cost-and-accuracy DSPy pipeline is itself an architectural-separation-adjacent proposal: rather than fixing a single model for the whole pipeline, it searches over which calls can use cheaper models/fewer retries while holding accuracy constant, yielding 53% (GPT-3.5) / 41% (Llama-3-70B) variable-cost reductions on HotPotQA at preserved accuracy, with fixed optimization overhead amortizing to near-zero after ~1,350 task uses. — [arXiv:2407.01502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)
- In the financial-document-extraction study (arXiv:2603.22651, lower-confidence source per above caveat), the "Hierarchical-Optimized" configuration — combining model routing with semantic caching and adaptive retry on top of a supervisor-worker separation — recovered 89% of the sequential-to-reflexive accuracy gap at only 1.15× the cheapest (sequential) baseline's cost, versus the pure reflexive loop's 2.30× cost for full accuracy — a concrete (if single-study) quantification of "separation + routing" beating "monolithic reasoning-heavy loop" on cost-efficiency. — [arXiv:2603.22651](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22651)

### Inferences
- There is a consistent architectural idea across both rigorous (ReWOO, LLMCompiler, DSPy joint-optimization) and practitioner sources: put a cheap, fast model (or deterministic/lightweight decision layer) in front to handle routing/tool-selection/task decomposition, and spend expensive-model tokens only on the step(s) that actually require strong reasoning (final synthesis, complex sub-tasks, or escalation on failure). This is converging into something like a de facto best practice, even though formal, benchmarked proof specifically isolating "cheap selector + expensive synthesizer" (as opposed to the closely related "planner-executor" or "model routing" framings) is still sparse in peer-reviewed venues.
- The magnitude of savings reported (40–90% depending on source) is wide enough, and the primary-vs-practitioner source mix uneven enough, that a workshop paper should present this as a promising, consistently-observed-but-not-yet-tightly-benchmarked direction rather than citing any single percentage as definitive.

### Gaps
- Could not extract exact quantitative tables from "Efficient Agents" (arXiv:2508.02694) due to PDF rendering limitations in the fetch tool; the qualitative claims (routing, planner-executor separation, tool-selection filtering) are confirmed, but specific accuracy/cost percentages from this paper are a gap — recommend the report-writer or a follow-up fetch obtain the HTML version (arxiv.org/html/2508.02694) if more precise numbers are needed.
- No peer-reviewed paper was found that isolates "cheap model for tool selection, expensive model only for final synthesis" as its own ablated variable (i.e., holding everything else constant and varying only this one architectural choice) — the evidence for this specific split is inferred from papers whose main contribution is broader (ReWOO's distillation result, OPPO's multi-strategy "Efficient Agents" paper) plus non-peer-reviewed practitioner blogs.
- The practitioner-source percentage claims (70-80%, 54%, 50-90% savings) come from vendor/blog content without disclosed benchmarks or methodology, and are not cross-validated against each other on a shared task — treat as directional signal only.
